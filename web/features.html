What a feature is

Real pictures · features you compute

A is a number computed from a stimulus: how bright a picture is, how loud a sound is, how many faces a photo holds. You'll compute some features yourself in this page, then compare them with features that trained models compute. Neuroscientists use numbers like these to ask which properties of a stimulus a brain region responds to.

Compute four features yourself

3

Sounds have features too

Play a clip from the open film Tears of Steel, or use your own voice. Your browser computes three sound features as it listens.

Loudness –Pitch –

Spectrogram (top): the sound split into frequencies, low at the bottom and high at the top. Stronger colour means more energy at that frequency. Speech shows stacked bands (harmonics). Thunder smears energy everywhere.

Loudness (middle) is the size of the waveform, in decibels below full scale. Pitch (bottom) is how many times per second the waveform repeats. It only exists while the sound repeats, so noise has none. In the film clips pitch appears only in patches, because music and room sound are mixed under every voice. Hum into the microphone to see a clean pitch line.

The microphone is used only while the button is on. Its sound is analysed in your browser and is never recorded or sent anywhere.

Features a model has to learn

Nobody can write a short recipe for "is there a face?" or "what kind of place is this?". Instead, a is trained on many labelled examples until it gives the right answers. Then it can score new pictures. These numbers were computed in advance by the open-source package viz2psy, for the picture you picked in step 1.
