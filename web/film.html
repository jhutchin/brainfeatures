Features over time

Real film · computed features

A film is a stream of images, sounds and words. Computer models can turn that stream into numbers: how bright the picture is, how many faces it holds, how loud the sound is, how surprising each word is. Each number, measured every half second, is a feature. Play the film and watch its features change with it.

How the features were made. Every feature was computed from the full-resolution film with the open-source packages viz2psy (pictures), aud2psy (sound) and word2psy (words), on a half-second grid, and collected by psytwill. The words come from a speech-recognition model, not the script, so some lines are missing or wrong. Models make mistakes: when a lane disagrees with what you see, the model is usually the one that is wrong.

Embeddings. Some models describe a moment with hundreds of numbers at once (an embedding). This page never shows those numbers; it shows only a two-dimensional map of how similar the moments are (multidimensional scaling).

Models behind this page, and their licences

Model weights are not redistributed here; the page shows only numbers the models computed on an openly licensed film. Several models carry non-commercial, research-use or share-alike terms, which this free, non-commercial teaching site follows.
